    - **The fix is one new XS function, `para_release_cv_depth()`, and one call per override in `disable()`.** It asks the **stacks** whether a frame is live - walking the running context plus every fiber's cxstack - and only then clears the count, re-canonicalises the landing pad at depth 1, and **purges the registry entry**. All three parts are load-bearing:
      - **The stacks, not the registry, is the only sound safety check.** The registry is a cache drained on every resume, and a frame that leaves without a swap in between - a cancelled or timed-out fiber body longjmp'ing out, a die unwinding a cxstack - never drains its claim. The first version gated on `e->max_depth > 0` and that is exactly what broke on Windows: the cache was stale, the release was vetoed, and the croak came straight back. Proven by planting a bogus claim, not argued: with the registry gate, `t/354_transparent.t` declines twice, croaks once and fails; with the stack scan, it releases twice and passes, 5/5.
      - **A live frame is still refused.** The CV is about to be finalised out from under it, so perl's own croak is the right answer; forcing the count down would leave a live frame pointing at freed memory.
      - **The purge is not optional.** `Pass 1b` pins `CvDEPTH` back to `max_depth` for every still-active entry, so a stale claim left in place resurrects the value on the very next swap - the croak would simply reappear one swap later.
